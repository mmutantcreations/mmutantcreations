Painted Music

Jonny Hawkins

Seeing Nothing More on stage felt like witnessing a pulse of pure talent move through the room — the kind of presence that doesn’t just perform, but reshapes the air around you. His music first wandered into my YouTube feed years ago like a quiet visitor, and from that moment it rooted itself in me. Experiencing him live was different, though; it was like watching emotion carve itself into sound. It stirred something in my own creative bones, urging me to step beyond the safe edges of my comfort zone and explore the deeper, more vulnerable terrain of my artistic expression.

Brent Smith

Seeing Shinedown live feels less like attending a concert and more like stepping into the gravitational pull of a g-force that knows exactly how to break the sound barrier. His voice doesn’t just fill space — it imprints on you, with the kind of conviction that makes you feel something in your ribs.
His music has been around for years and is one of the only truth-telling arts left lately. Witnessing him in person was inexplanable; it was like watching fire learn how to speak. His performance reminded me that emotion isn’t something to hide behind — it’s something to wield. It inspired me, as an artist who works in the language of feeling, to step further into my own vulnerability and let it sharpen my craft rather than soften it.
